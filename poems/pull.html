the steady pull

Some nights it will look like the end,
the letters stacked, the phone face-down,
the room gone quiet the way a house
goes quiet after someone leaves.
I have sat in those rooms before.
I know how the clock sounds
when it is the only thing still talking.
I know how a whole day can be folded
and put away in a drawer no one opens.
But I am alive.
And for as long as I am,
you are loved.
Not promised in a hurry,
not sworn at a door,
but learned, the way my hands learn
the weight of the cup I lift each morning.

Some mornings I lose the words
I meant to say to you.
The tea goes cold. The window fogs.
I still know what the words were for.
When I say your name, my voice softens,
the way it did the first time,
on a day I did not think I would be brave enough.
Whatever the years do to the rest of me,
that part stays soft.

The seasons may empty their hands.
The roads may close behind us.
The cafés will change their songs,
and the streets will learn new names.
I will still know where you are,
the way a river knows the sea,
not by seeing it,
but by a pull that has never once been wrong.
