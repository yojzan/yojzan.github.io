The Weight of the Unanswered

I keep finding stones inside my coat.
I never remember bringing them home.
Perhaps I wonder, if my hands keep
what my mind cannot answer.

The walk to school follows a river.
Every morning it passes
like something certain.
I have never envied people.
Only rivers.

They ask me what I will become.
The room answers before I can.
I leave my page blank,
for I do not understand the question.

How could I ever choose
what to become
before learning what I am?

The stones grow smooth.
Not because I let them go,
but because questions,
carried long enough,
begin to resemble answers.

If one day
my name becomes
another quiet thing,
I fear it will not be death
that empties it.

I fear
a life spent waiting
to understand
why I was given one,
only for the question
to leave before I did.
