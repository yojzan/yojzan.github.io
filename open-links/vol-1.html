ARGA

Letter in Rose and Wine

I am writing this with the lamp off so the window can glow pink from the sunset. Outside, the rose bushes have bled their last petals onto the path, and I keep thinking they look like words I cannot quite say. Maybe that is how you feel about me. Maybe I am a color you have not named yet.

You asked me once what I was afraid of. I said nothing, which was a lie wearing a pink dress. I am afraid of the ordinary hours. The kettle, the unopened mail, the way a room goes quiet when someone you love is about to leave it. I am afraid that love wears down to a habit, and that I will not notice until the red has faded to paper.

But here is what I know.

When you come in with the cold still on your coat, the whole house flushes toward the door. The clock stops being an enemy. I have counted the stairs on the nights you are late, and I have counted them again when you arrive, and the numbers never match, because the stairs are only stairs and it is my pulse that keeps changing its mind.

I do not want a tidy love. I do not want the kind that sends roses and forgets the vase. I want the kind that knows where you keep your anger and does not flinch when you take it out to look at it. I want to be the one you show your bruises to, and I want you to show me yours before I ask.

There are days I am sharp. I will cut the bread too thin and say something too true, and you will go quiet, and I will hate myself for the silence I made. Forgive the knife. It was never pointed at you. It was pointed at the part of me that thinks I have to earn the right to be held.

So here is the plain thing, stripped of the rose.

I love you, and I am not finished being surprised by it.

When the spring comes, and it will, I want to stand with you in the warm grass and watch the blossoms open one by one. I want to see what you look like when you are not performing for anyone. And I want you to see me the same way, rough edges and all.

Come in from the dusk. The lamp is still off. The window is still glowing.
